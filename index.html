Warning Signs

What to Look For

Scammers try to make you act quickly, feel afraid, or hand over information. These are the main warning signs in plain language.

Pressure and Urgency

Messages that say "act now", "within 24 hours", or "final warning" try to rush you so you do not think clearly.

Threats

Threats like "your account will be closed" or "you will lose access" are designed to scare you into acting.

Asking for Secrets

Real companies will never ask for your password, PIN, or verification code by email.

Money and Gift Cards

Legitimate organizations do not demand payment by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency.

Sneaky Links

Shortened links and raw IP-address links can hide where a link really goes. Hover (without clicking) to preview.

Attachments and Macros

Unexpected attachments, especially programs (.exe) or files asking you to enable macros, are dangerous.

Impersonation

Vague greetings like "Dear Customer" and claims from a fake "security team" are common tricks.

Surprise Invoices

Unexpected invoices or "overdue payment" notes often hide a scam inside the attachment or link.

Methodology

How the Risk Score Works

There is no mystery number. Every warning sign is worth a set number of points, and the total becomes your score (0-100). Each point is shown and explained in your results.

Low Risk (0-34)

Few or no warning signs found. Still, always double-check unexpected messages.

Suspicious (35-69)

Several warning signs. Treat the message with caution and verify it another way.

High Risk (70-100)

Many strong warning signs. Do not click links or open attachments. Verify officially.

Important: This tool can never say for certain whether an email is safe or malicious. It is an educational triage tool that highlights warning signs. A low score does not guarantee safety, and a high score does not prove harm, but a high score is a strong reason to stop and check.

What This Tool Cannot Do

  • It cannot open or inspect attachments.
  • It cannot visit links or check whether a linked website is actually malicious.
  • It only analyzes the text you paste; it cannot retrieve the real email or verify who sent it.
  • Its header checks (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) only run if you paste the full email headers.
  • Its score is a rule-based heuristic, not a guarantee. A low score is not proof of safety, and a high score is not proof of harm.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my email uploaded anywhere?

No. All analysis happens locally in your browser using JavaScript on this page. Your email text never leaves your device, and there is no server, account, or network request involved.

Can this tool tell me for certain if an email is a phishing attempt?

No. This is an educational triage tool. It highlights common warning signs and gives an explainable risk score, but it cannot give a definitive verdict. Always verify important messages independently.

What are SPF, DKIM, and DMARC?

They are email authentication checks. SPF verifies the sending server, DKIM verifies a digital signature, and DMARC ties the two together. If you paste the full email headers, the tool can look for failures in these checks.

Do I need to install anything or create an account?

No. This is a static website made of plain files. There is no sign-up, no database, and no software to install. It even works with your internet connection turned off.

What should I do if I get a High Risk result?

Do not click any links or open any attachments. Verify the message using the company's official website or app, or contact the sender using a number you already know and trust. When in doubt, delete the email and report it.

Is this a replacement for antivirus or email security software?

No. It is a learning and triage aid, not a security product. It only looks at the text you paste and cannot scan attachments, visit links, or protect your device.
